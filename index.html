Lyydia Alajääskö

I am an independent and driven young woman with a passion for learning, striving to better myself and the society around me. I work at the intersection of behavioral genetics and socio-economics — a positioning that brings me close to philosophical and ethical questions that occupy my mind a great deal these days. I notice human differences, and this has always intrigued me: I am curious what makes us who we are, I care about inequality, and I always think about implications. I am currently finalising my PhD thesis and building the path ahead. Born into a multilingual and multicultural environment and educated across diverse cultural and academic contexts, I tend to live as an outsider looking in. My roots are in the land of the midnight sun, but my footsteps are visible across borders, in between nations. I am not yet sure who I am — rather, I am continuously constructing myself within my scattered world, learning to approach myself and others with tenderness and kindness. Photography found me through my dad's old analog camera. In an age when we can snap countless photos with a single tap, shooting on film feels meaningful to me. I believe there are many ways of knowing — where research offers confidence within a very small margin, an image or a poem can express something far greater with far fewer words. My simple hope is that my photography evokes emotion.
